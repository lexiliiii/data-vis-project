The US didn't stop buying from China. It started buying from everyone else.

China supplied 21.9% of US goods imports in 2017. By 2025 it supplied 9.4%. Mexico, Vietnam and Taiwan took much of that share. For sourcing teams the change matters less as a drop than as a new map of who the US depends on.

Rising insightSketches 4, 10

Twenty years ago the top three were Canada, China and Mexico. Mexico now leads.

Ranked by total trade (imports plus exports), the top of the table held steady for a decade. Then it moved. Mexico passed China in 2019 and passed Canada in 2023. Taiwan climbed from 11th to 4th in eight years, likely driven by AI-related semiconductors. Vietnam was 41st in 2006 and is 7th today.

Rising insightSketches 21, 1

China's decline is concentrated in a few product groups.

China's share fell fastest in electronics, machinery and furniture. In toys, China still supplies more than half of US imports. The largest US import groups, like vehicles, fuels and pharmaceuticals, were never mainly sourced from China, so the headline drop says less about them.

Rising insightSketch 29

US imports from China peaked at $576 billion in 2022, then fell 43% by 2025.

Imports from China kept growing after the 2018 tariffs and recovered after COVID-19. The steep fall came after 2022. Exports to China moved much less, so the trade deficit with China shrank from $422 billion in 2022 to $221 billion in 2025.

Policy markers show what happened at the same time. They don't show what caused the change.
