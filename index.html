CS 6730 · Team Viz Lab US goods trade · 2006–2025

The US didn't stop buying from China.It started buying from everyone else.

China supplied 21.9% of US goods imports in 2017. By 2025 it supplied 9.4%. Mexico, Vietnam and Taiwan took much of that share. For sourcing teams the change matters less as a drop than as a new map of who the US depends on.

01 · The overviewWhere did China's share go?

The headline is a fall in China's share. The larger story is a rewiring of the supplier network. Brush a period below to carry the same years through the rest of the story.

02 · A new orderTrading-partner rank

Twenty years ago the top three were Canada, China and Mexico. Mexico now leads.

Ranked by total trade (imports plus exports), the top of the table held steady for a decade. Then it moved. Mexico passed China in 2019 and passed Canada in 2023. Taiwan climbed from 11th to 4th in eight years, likely driven by AI-related semiconductors. Vietnam was 41st in 2006 and is 7th today. Ranks 11+ groups every position outside the top ten on one baseline.

Dependency did not disappear. It changed addresses.

04 · The turning pointUS–China goods trade

US imports from China peaked at $576 billion in 2022, then fell 43% by 2025.

Imports from China kept growing after the 2018 tariffs and recovered after COVID-19. The steep fall came after 2022. Exports to China moved much less, so the trade deficit with China shrank from $422 billion in 2022 to $221 billion in 2025. This simplified line-and-area view replaces the crowded paired bars.

Policy markers show what happened at the same time. They don't show what caused the change.
